# Damocles — expected ability scores per tier

Companion to the mechanical brief. Same marking convention: unmarked = corpus,
**[CLAUDE]** = my modelling choice, **[UNVERIFIED]** = believed but not found.

---

## Inputs, from the corpus

- **Standard array:** 15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8, assigned freely. Optional
  "Standard Array +2" adds +2 to one score. (4d6-drop-lowest is also offered.)
- **Bloodline modifiers are fixed** — no floating stats. Typical shape is
  +2 / +1 / +1, sometimes with a −1 (Tammeni is CON+1 STR+1 INT+2 DEX−1;
  Homunculus is CON+3; Bilupine is STR+1 CON+1).
- **Ability Score Improvement** is a repeatable feat: "+2 to one score, or +1 to
  two." **No maximum is stated anywhere in the corpus.**
- **Every other feat is a half-feat**: +1 to one of the abilities it lists.
- **Tier feats** at 4, 7, 10, 13, 16, 19, 22, 25, 28 — nine, one per TB step.
- **Vocation feats**: most vocations 4/8/12/16 (four). Warrior 4/6/8/12/14/16
  (six). Revenant, Tinker, Rogue (five). Monk 8/12/16 (three).
- **Epic boons** at 20 and 30; the level-30 one replaces a feat. Not counted as
  ability points here.

So the baseline character has **13 feats by level 30**. A Warrior has 15, a Monk
12. Tables below use the 13-feat baseline.

**[UNVERIFIED]** modifier = (score − 10) ÷ 2, rounded down. I could not find the
rule text. If Damocles uses a different curve the score columns still hold and
every modifier column needs redoing.

---

## The four profiles — **[CLAUDE]**, these are my archetypes, not corpus categories

The rules only say "+2 to one, or +1 to two". Everything below is a modelling
choice about how a player spends that.

| | bloodline | feat spend |
|---|---|---|
| **ALL IN** | chosen so its +2 lands on the primary | every feat an ASI, every point into the primary |
| **GOOD** | +2 on the primary | ~3 build feats whose listed ability is the primary or CON; rest ASI, ~70% primary / 20% CON / 10% weakest save |
| **GENERALIST** | +2 on the primary | ~35% primary, 20% CON, rest spread; prefers +1/+1 ASIs |
| **POOR** | chosen for flavour, +2 lands on an unused stat | build feats bump abilities the character never rolls; points scattered; dump stat kept at 8 |

Points banked per feat: ALL IN 2.0, GOOD 1.85, GENERALIST 1.7, POOR 1.4 — the
gap is build feats giving +1 where an ASI gives +2.

Columns: **PRI** = the attack or casting stat. **SEC** = second-most-used.
**d / e / f** = the rest. Rows are the level at which each TB step arrives.

**The gap between an optimised and an unoptimised primary widens from 1 to 11
across the level range**, because ability scores are uncapped and feats keep
arriving. On a d20 an 11-point spread is more than half the die.

| 1 | 1 | 12 | 17 | 13+ | 13+ | 13+ | 14+ |
| 2 | 4 | 14 | 19 | 12+ | 13+ | 13+ | 15+ |
| 3 | 7 | 16 | 21 | 12+ | 13+ | 14+ | 15+ |
| 4 | 10 | 18 | 23 | 11+ | 13+ | 15+ | 16+ |
| 5 | 13 | 20 | 25 | 10+ | 12+ | 15+ | 16+ |
| 6 | 16 | 22 | 27 | 9+ | 12+ | 15+ | 17+ |
| 7 | 19 | 24 | 29 | 9+ | 13+ | 16+ | 18+ |
| 8 | 22 | 26 | 31 | 9+ | 13+ | 17+ | 18+ |
| 9 | 25 | 28 | 33 | 9+ | 13+ | 17+ | 18+ |
| 10 | 28 | 30 | 35 | 9+ | 14+ | 18+ | 20+ |

**The band tracks GOOD almost exactly** — a well-built character needs 12–14
against the hardest thing in the band at every tier, which is a stable and
sensible target. ALL IN drifts to a 9+ and stays there; GENERALIST and POOR fall
off the bottom, needing 18+ and 20+ by TB 10.

**[CLAUDE]** So the band is not badly calibrated — it is calibrated to one
profile, and the uncapped stat race is what spreads the other three away from it
in both directions.

## Constitution

| TB | lvl | ALL IN | GOOD | GENERALIST | POOR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | +2 | +2 | +2 | +1 |
| 4 | 10 | +2 | +3 | +3 | +1 |
| 6 | 16 | +2 | +4 | +4 | +2 |
| 10 | 28 | **+2** | +4 | +4 | +2 |

Constitution is added to **every Hit Die spent**, so it prices the healing
economy as well as Constitution saves.

**[CLAUDE]** Note the ALL IN column: a character who puts every point into one
stat has +2 Constitution at level 28. Hit Dice are the only non-magical healing,
so the all-in profile is buying its accuracy directly out of the party's
attrition budget. That is a real cost the accuracy tables do not show, and it is
the closest thing to a natural brake on the all-in profile that I can find.

## Armour Class, for the Poise conversation

Light armour is 11–12 + Dex, **uncapped**. Medium caps Dex at +2 (best: Halfplate
15 + 2 = 17). Heavy has no Dex at all (best: Plate 18, + Greatshield 3 = **21**).

| TB | lvl | band centre | gambeson 12+Dex, Dex all-in | medium (cap) | plate + greatshield |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 12 | 15 | 17 | 21 |
| 2 | 4 | 14 | 17 | 17 | 21 |
| 3 | 7 | 16 | 18 | 17 | 21 |
| 4 | 10 | 18 | 20 | 17 | 21 |
| 5 | 13 | 20 | 22 | 17 | 21 |
| 6 | 16 | 22 | 24 | 17 | 21 |
| 7 | 19 | 24 | 25 | 17 | 21 |
| 8 | 22 | 26 | 26 | 17 | 21 |
| 9 | 25 | 28 | 27 | 17 | 21 |
| 10 | 28 | 30 | **28** | **17** | **21** |

**[CLAUDE] The shape I would put in front of the Poise conversation.** There are
three different Armour Class curves and one band:

- **flat at 21** (heavy), from the moment you can afford Plate until level 30
- **capped at 17** (medium), from about level 4 onward
- **an uncapped stat** (light), which is the only one that tracks the band — and
  it tracks it because it is riding the *same* uncapped stat race that drives the
  accuracy column above

By TB 10 a heavy-armour character is at 21 against attackers with +15 to +26. A
light-armour character who went all in on Dexterity is at 28 and roughly keeping
pace, but has spent their entire build on it, and has +2 Constitution.

So the current defensive maths asks a player to choose between an AC that stops
growing at level 5 and an AC that only grows if they spend the whole character on
it. That asymmetry — not the absolute numbers — is what I would want any new
defensive layer measured against.

## Caveats

- **[CLAUDE]** The profile weights are invented. The corpus constrains only "+2
  or +1/+1" per ASI and "+1 to a listed ability" per half-feat.
- Scores move in steps of 2 in these tables; a real sheet will show odd values
  from half-feats, which do not change the modifier until they pair up.
- The 13-feat baseline is the common vocation. Warrior gets 15 (add roughly +1 to
  +2 to the primary modifier at the top end); Monk gets 12.
- Bloodline modifiers are approximated as a generic +2/+1/+1. Real bloodlines
  vary, including negatives — Tammeni's DEX−1 for instance.
- Magic items, heirlooms and epic boons are not modelled at all.
- Generated by `stats_per_tier.py`; change `WEIGHTS`, `POINTS_PER_FEAT` or
  `BASE` and everything regenerates.
