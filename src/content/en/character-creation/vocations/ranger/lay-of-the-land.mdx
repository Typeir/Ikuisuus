# Lay Of The Land

A lay of the land is a specific prey or path that you have studied and understand.
Each lay of the land provides benefits according to your ranger levels and the specialization you choose.

## Prey

A prey is a specific type of creature that you have studied and understand. You can choose a prey from the following list:

- Aberrations
- Beasts
- Monstrosities
- Constructs
- Celestials
- Plants
- Humanoids
- Elementals

Some preys have specific subtypes, if the creature has a subtype, you do not gain the benefits of the prey against that creature unless you have chosen the specific subtype as your prey.
For example, if you choose "Humanoids (Dwarves)" as your prey, you do not gain the benefits of that prey against a human or a gnome.

Whenever you pick a Prey with a subtype, you can pick a different subtype of the same or other Prey as part of that selection.
For example, you could choose "Humanoids (Dwarves)" and "Humanoids (Humans)" as part of a single lay of the land selection.
A common subtype is "X (Ooze)" or "X (Undead)", which applies to any creature of type X that has the ooze or undead subtype, respectively.
Most undead are constructs.

## Path

A path is a specific type of terrain that you have studied and understand. You can choose a path from the following list:

- Frigid (An environment with extreme cold, such as arctic tundras or icy mountains)
- Mountainous (An environment characterized by high elevations, rugged terrain, and often rocky landscapes)
- Swampy (An environment dominated by wetlands, marshes, and bogs, often with dense vegetation and standing water)
- Arid (An environment with very little rainfall, such as deserts or dry plains, or an area that is otherwise dry and barren)
- Plain (An environment characterized by flat, open land with few trees or other features, such as grasslands or savannas)
- Forested (An environment covered in dense trees and undergrowth, such as jungles or temperate forests)
- Coastal (An environment adjacent to or near the ocean, including beaches, cliffs, and tidal zones)
- Cavernous (An environment consisting of caves, underground tunnels, and subterranean spaces)
- Urban (An environment characterized by human-made structures, buildings, and cityscapes)
- Volcanic (An environment dominated by volcanic activity, including lava flows, ash fields, and geothermal features)
- Ethereal (An environment dominated by a lack of features, such as outer space, derelict astral objects, or worlds with few common features)
- Windswept (An environment characterized by strong, persistent winds, often found floating at high altitudes)
- Aquatic (An environment dominated by water, such as oceans, lakes, or deep underwater regions)
